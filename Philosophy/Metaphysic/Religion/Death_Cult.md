
## Death cult

A **death cult** is a pejorative term for a 
religious, ideological, or social system in which 
**death, dying, or self-sacrifice is given an 
unusually high positive moral, spiritual, or symbolic value**.

Typically, such a system may:

- **Glorify death** as noble, sacred, heroic, or transformative.
    
- Regard **dying for the group, faith, leader, or cause as a supreme virtue**.
    
- Treat **martyrdom or self-sacrifice** as preferable to compromise, surrender, or continued life.
    
- Portray ordinary life as **inferior, corrupt, meaningless, or less important** than the cause.
    
- Promise some form of **honor, salvation, immortality, purification, or transcendence through death**.
    
- Devalue the instinct for self-preservation when it conflicts with the group's ideals.
    

### Important distinction

**“Death cult” is not a neutral academic classification.** 
It is generally used critically to characterize a group 
whose ideology is perceived as excessively death-oriented.

For example:

> **“Life is preferable to death, except when living entails dishonor; an honorable death is therefore preferable to a dishonorable life.”**

That belief **by itself does not necessarily constitute a death cult**. It could instead be described as an **honor-based death ethic** or **martyrdom ethic**.

A stronger death-cult characterization would be appropriate when **death itself becomes a central positive objective**, rather than merely an unfortunate consequence of defending one's honor or principles.
